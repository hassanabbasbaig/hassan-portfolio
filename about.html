About

An economist interested in the systems behind good decisions.

My career has moved across monetary policy, inflation expectations, large-scale surveys, macroeconomic forecasting, policy frameworks, financial regulation, administrative records, spatial data and modern data systems. The subjects change, but the underlying problem is often similar: an institution has an important decision to make and the existing evidence or analytical process is not yet good enough.

That is the part of the work I find most interesting. I tend to focus not only on producing an answer, but on the capability required to produce good answers repeatedly: the measurement process, model, dataset, workflow, governance structure or communication system that makes analysis dependable.

At the State Bank of Pakistan, that meant work on expectations measurement, recurring surveys, macroeconomic forecasting, scenario analysis and policy-support systems. At the University of Freiburg, it meant building reproducible research workflows that could turn fragmented administrative records, geography and weather data into analysis-ready empirical infrastructure.

Economics remains my disciplinary core. It provides the language for thinking about incentives, constraints, expectations, trade-offs, transmission mechanisms and uncertainty. Econometrics, programming, survey design and data engineering are tools I use to make those questions tractable.

I am most useful in environments where consequential decisions meet fragmented evidence—central banks, regulators, public institutions, research organisations, international institutions and analytical or strategy teams.
