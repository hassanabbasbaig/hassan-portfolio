About

An applied economist connecting economic questions with quantitative evidence.

My professional background combines central banking and academic research. At the State Bank of Pakistan, I worked across monetary policy, macroeconomic forecasting, international economic analysis, financial stability, survey research and applied econometrics. At the University of Freiburg, I developed reproducible R and Stata workflows for microeconometric research.

A recurring part of my work is translating an economic question into an empirical or modelling strategy. Depending on the problem, that has meant integrating sectoral forecasts into medium-term macroeconomic scenarios, estimating monetary-policy transmission with very large supervisory lending datasets, designing survey methodology and data systems, or using machine-learning methods to extend high-frequency forecasting.

My current master’s thesis research extends that approach to long-run institutional change after natural disasters. The research combines multilingual news, household and individual survey data, structured events, geospatial evidence, climate and satellite sources. To support it, I have built a reusable knowledge-discovery platform over more than 2.8 million articles and harmonised more than 1.4 million survey observations.

The quantitative toolkit is broad—QPM/IRIS, SVARs, time-series and panel econometrics, machine learning, Python, R, Stata, MATLAB, SQL, geospatial analysis, graph databases and vector retrieval—but the methods are instruments for answering economic and policy questions rather than ends in themselves.
